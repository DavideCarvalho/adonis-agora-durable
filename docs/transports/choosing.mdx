Every transport carries the same `RemoteTask` / `StepResult` JSON, and the engine's durable machinery (checkpoints, replay, retry backoff, timers, flow-control admission, dead-lettering) runs on top of any of them. The transports differ in everything **around** that contract: how fast a task is picked up, how many run at once, how a crashed worker is detected, and which optional `Transport` capabilities they implement. The engine turns some cluster features on only when the transport implements them.

This page compares the five drivers side by side. Every cell reflects what the transport's source does today.

## Capability matrix

✅ supported · ⚠️ partial or conditional (see the numbered note) · ❌ not supported · n/a does not apply

`queue` is [`transports.queue()`](/docs/durable/transports/queue) over `@adonisjs/queue`, on its Redis adapter or a database (Knex) adapter. The two adapters implement the same contract the transport uses, so a cell differs between them only where it says so. `memory` is the in-process test driver (see [Testing](/docs/durable/testing)); it's listed for completeness, not for production.

### Reliability

| | `bullmq` | `queue` | `db` (Lucid) | `eventEmitter` | `memory` (tests) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Delivery guarantee | At-least-once | At-least-once | At-least-once | At-most-once (lost with the process) | At-most-once |
| Detects a crashed worker via | BullMQ job lock + a peer worker's stalled check⁴ | Claim renewal + the reclaim sweep⁴ | Lease expiry on the next claim poll⁴ | ❌ | ❌ |
| Default time to re-deliver a crashed worker's step | About 30–60 s (30 s lock, stalled check every 30 s) | About 2–2.5 min (`stalledThresholdMs` 120 s, plus up to one 30 s sweep) | About 30 s (`leaseMs`), plus one poll | ❌ | ❌ |
| Claim or lock renewed while a long step runs | ✅ BullMQ extends the job lock | ✅ `renewJobs` every half `stalledThresholdMs`, on 0.6 and 0.7⁵ | ✅ every held claim, every `leaseMs / 3`⁵ | n/a | n/a |
| Poison bound (worker keeps dying ➜ failed `StepResult` ➜ durable retry) | ✅ the `failed` bridge, once a job stalls past BullMQ's `maxStalledCount` (default 1, so on the second stall) | ⚠️ `maxStalledCount` (3), `@adonisjs/queue` 0.7 only⁶ | ✅ `maxStalledCount` (3) | ❌ | ❌ |
| Result redelivered when the engine's handler throws | ✅ 5 attempts, exponential backoff from 500 ms, then kept 24 h and reported | ✅ redelivered one poll interval later, with no cap | ⚠️ redelivered after `leaseMs`, with no cap | ✅ in-memory, backoff 100 ms → 5 s, until `close()`⁷ | ❌ |

### Operations

| | `bullmq` | `queue` | `db` (Lucid) | `eventEmitter` | `memory` (tests) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Infrastructure to run | Redis, plus the `bullmq` and `ioredis` packages | `@adonisjs/queue` and its backend (Redis, or a database through Knex), plus the app database's worker-key table for health and advertisement | The database you already run, through `@adonisjs/lucid` (transport tables auto-created or migrated) | None | None |
| Several engine instances | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ⚠️ only when every step is `ctx.localStep`¹² | ❌ |
| Built-in control plane (cross-pod live-tail and `cancel` fan-out) | ❌ pair with `controlPlanes.redis`¹³ | ❌ pair with `controlPlanes.redis`¹³ | ❌ pair with `controlPlanes.redis`¹³ | ⚠️ local to the process | ⚠️ local to the process |
| Bounded shutdown | ✅ `closeTimeoutMs` (default 5 s) | ✅ `closeTimeoutMs` (default 5 s) | ✅ `closeTimeoutMs` (default 5 s) | Nothing to close | Nothing to close |

**Notes**

1. Polling is the cost of a backend-agnostic transport. A `queue` worker runs one task loop per step name it serves, so a worker serving 30 names runs 30 loops; `db` runs one task loop for all of them. Every engine also polls the results table or queue every 200 ms, and the step-event and protocol loops it starts. On the Knex adapter, or on `db`, that load lands on your database.
2. Every `emit` starts its handler at once, so there is no limit. A burst of steps runs all at once in the same process.
3. `@adonisjs/queue` runs the lowest number first on a 1–10 scale (default 5). The transport sends `5 − priority`, clamped, so an unprioritised step sits at 5, but every priority of 4 or more lands on 1 and every priority of −5 or less lands on 10. Only priorities from −4 to 4 stay distinct.
4. Recovery needs a **live consumer of that step name**. BullMQ's stalled check runs on a worker of the same queue, the `queue` reclaim sweep walks only the queues its instance pops from, and a `db` worker claims only rows for the step names it serves. A restarted worker counts. If the only worker for a step dies and never comes back, nothing settles the step; the engine's opt-in `remoteRedispatchMs` covers that case on every transport (see [Lost remote dispatch](/docs/durable/reliability/failure-modes#lost-remote-dispatch-the-run-suspends-and-nothing-auto-redrives-it)).
5. Renewal runs on a timer, so a step that blocks the event loop longer than the threshold (`stalledThresholdMs` on `queue`, `leaseMs` on `db`) looks dead and is re-delivered while it still runs. Both transports report the lost claim to `onError`. Keep heavy work async, or raise the threshold. An adapter without `renewJobs` (only a custom one) gets no renewal on `queue`, and the threshold defaults to 30 minutes there.
6. On `@adonisjs/queue` 0.7 the adapter hands a job that stalled too often back to the transport, which fails it and publishes a retryable failed result. On 0.6 the adapter deletes such a job inside its own sweep and returns only a count, so no result is published and the step waits. Set the step's `timeoutMs` or the engine's `remoteRedispatchMs` there. See [Poison jobs](/docs/durable/transports/queue#poison-jobs).
7. A failing result handler (a store timeout while settling, say) is reported to `onError` and redelivered from the in-memory copy with backoff. The copy dies with the process — an emitter result is never on a broker — so a crash between the failure and the retry still loses it; recovery then re-drives the step from its checkpoint.
8. On `bullmq` every engine instance hears every beat. On `queue` and `db` a beat is a job or a row that one instance takes. Before a `timeoutMs` step's window expires, the dispatching instance reads the last beat persisted on the checkpoint, and keeps waiting if another instance recorded a recent one. That needs a store that persists beats (`recordStepHeartbeat`, which the Lucid store implements) and allows up to `heartbeatPersistThrottleMs` (10 s) of extra silence once beats land elsewhere.
9. `<P>-decisions` (`bullmq`) and `<prefix>:decisions` (`queue`) are point-to-point queues that every engine consumes. With several engine replicas, a decision taken by a replica that didn't dispatch the turn finds no waiter and is dropped, and the turn waits for the executor's `timeoutMs` or for run recovery to re-drive it. With one engine-side instance this never happens.
10. Live-tail events need a worker-key store on the control plane. Replies and events are polled, and a new subscription can miss the first second or so of events. See [Tenant pods over the queue transport](/docs/durable/transports/queue#tenant-pods-over-the-queue-transport).
11. The worker advertisement and liveness keys live in a key store beside the queues, by default the app database's `durable_transport_worker_keys` table. With `workerKeys: false`, or no database, the transport doesn't implement `groupHealth` and the panels stay empty; a `tenant` pod without one forwards its advertisement to the control plane. `depth` counts only pending jobs because the `@adonisjs/queue` adapter contract has no count for jobs in flight or delayed retries. See [Worker health](/docs/durable/transports/queue#worker-health).
12. Replicas sharing `eventEmitter` and one Lucid store never run the same run, because leases, checkpoints, signals and cancellation all go through the store. A `ctx.step` is still handled only by the process that dispatched it. See [Pick a topology](/docs/durable/production#2-pick-a-topology).
13. `bullmq` doesn't implement `ControlPlane` at all. `queue` and `db` implement `publishControl` / `onControl`, but over a single-consumer queue (`<prefix>:control`) or table (`durable_transport_control`), so each message reaches one instance, not every one. Use [`controlPlanes.redis`](/docs/durable/transports/control-plane) for cross-pod fan-out with any of them.

**The same on every transport, because the engine owns it:** checkpoints and replay, retry backoff (a durable `wakeAt` on the failed checkpoint), run leases and run recovery, timers, flow-control admission priority and fairness, dead-lettering, `remoteRedispatchMs`, namespaces, and failover across a [transport pool](/docs/durable/transports#pinning-a-transport-per-step-failover-pools). They behave the same whichever transport carries the step.

## Limitations per transport

**`bullmq`.** It needs Redis and two extra packages. Crash recovery depends on a live worker of the same queue, and a clean shutdown doesn't hand unfinished jobs back: after `closeTimeoutMs` they wait for BullMQ's stalled check. The failure bridge exists only in consumers that implement it (this transport, the NestJS one and the Python `durable-worker`). With several engine replicas, a remote workflow's decision can land on the wrong replica (note 9). There is no control plane in the transport, so add `controlPlanes.redis`.

**`queue`.** It's polling-based, so a first task after a quiet spell can wait up to `idleBackoffMax × pollIntervalMs`, and every served step name costs one poll loop. Crash detection is the slowest of the three (about 2 minutes by default). The poison bound needs `@adonisjs/queue` 0.7. Priority is squeezed onto a 1–10 scale. Health depth counts pending jobs only, and health and descriptors need a worker-key store. Heartbeats and decisions are single-consumer (notes 8 and 9), there's no control plane, and there's no cross-language wire.

**`db` (Lucid).** It's polling-based, and every loop, renewal and liveness write is a query against the database your store also uses, so throughput is bounded by polling and row contention. A result whose engine-side handling throws waits out `leaseMs` before it's redelivered. Heartbeats are single-consumer (note 8). There's no control plane, and no cross-language wire: Python and NestJS workers speak `bullmq` only.

**`eventEmitter`.** Single process for steps. There's no persistence of in-flight steps (a crash loses them; the run itself resumes from its checkpoints), no concurrency limit and no worker health. A dispatched step with no registered handler is ignored silently, because another subscriber might own it, so the run waits on it.

**`memory`.** For tests. It runs the handler inside `dispatch`, needs `onResult` registered first, and carries no heartbeats.

## Which one to pick

<Callout type="info" title="Recommended for production: bullmq">
`transports.bullmq()` is the most complete transport:

- **Push delivery.** A BullMQ worker blocks on Redis instead of polling, so pickup is milliseconds and an idle fleet costs a few held connections, not a stream of queries.
- **The only cross-language wire.** It is byte-compatible with the NestJS BullMQ transport and the Python `durable-worker`, so Python steps, Python `@workflow` bodies and NestJS workers share one Redis with your Adonis engine. See [Python interop](/docs/durable/python) and [interop](/docs/durable/cluster/interop).
- **Native lock renewal and stall handling.** BullMQ extends the job lock while a step runs, and a peer's stalled check re-queues a crashed worker's job. A job that keeps stalling past BullMQ's limit is failed, and the transport bridges that into a retryable `StepResult`. A result whose engine-side handling throws is retried 5 times with backoff. Recovery takes about 30–60 seconds.
- **The broadest dashboard depth.** Worker health counts waiting, active, delayed and prioritized jobs, and step heartbeats reach every engine instance over pub/sub.

Pair it with `controlPlanes.redis` for cross-pod live-tail and cancellation. If you can run Redis, use `bullmq`.
</Callout>

**`queue`** makes sense when the app already runs `@adonisjs/queue` and you want durable steps on the same backend and the same `config/queue.ts`, or when you want a database-backed queue through its Knex adapter with Adonis-style configuration. Run `@adonisjs/queue` 0.7 for the poison bound, keep the default worker-key table for health and descriptors, and add `controlPlanes.redis` when you run more than one engine instance.

**`db`** makes sense when you have no Redis at all and want the database you already run to be the queue. It fits a modest dispatch rate (workflow and pipeline scale rather than a high-fanout firehose) and idempotent steps. It is also the only transport that addresses a remote workflow's decision to the engine that dispatched it.

**`eventEmitter`** is for a single process: a monolith, local development or a first deploy. It needs no infrastructure. Several replicas can share it with a Lucid store when every step is a `ctx.localStep` (see [Pick a topology](/docs/durable/production#2-pick-a-topology)). Moving to a broker later is a config change, not a workflow-code change.

**`memory`** is for tests only.

You don't have to pick only one. The engine accepts an ordered [pool of transports](/docs/durable/transports#pinning-a-transport-per-step-failover-pools) with failover and per-step pinning.
