Durable execution is a crowded space, and the honest answer to "which one?" is *it depends on your
architecture*. This page positions `@adonis-agora/durable` against the tools people most often weigh
it against — starting with the one every AdonisJS app already has, `@adonisjs/queue` — as fairly as we
can manage.

The short version: `@adonis-agora/durable` is a **library, not a platform**. It lives inside your AdonisJS
app as a provider, persists state to the SQL database you already run through [Lucid](/docs/durable/stores/lucid),
and dispatches steps over the queue you already have — or over that same database, with no broker at all.
There is no extra server to deploy, no vendor to sign up with, and no new language runtime — the trade
being that *you* own scaling and operations, the way you already do for the rest of your app.

## vs plain `@adonisjs/queue` jobs

This is the comparison that matters most in practice, because `@adonisjs/queue` is what an AdonisJS app
reaches for first — and this library **runs happily on top of it** as its cross-process
[transport](/docs/durable/transports/queue).

A queue gives you jobs: a class under `app/jobs/` with an `execute()`, dispatched to a worker. What it does
not give you is a *durable function*: there is no replay, so a multi-step process must be modeled as a
chain of independent jobs passing state through their payloads. The "what happened so far" lives
implicitly across jobs; a crash between them leaves you reconstructing progress; waiting on a human
approval or a payment callback means inventing a persistence scheme for the half-finished flow.

`@adonis-agora/durable` keeps the queue underneath for what it's great at — moving step invocations
between processes — and adds the durable layer on top: the whole flow is one readable `run(ctx, input)`,
every step is checkpointed, waits are `await ctx.sleep(...)` / `await ctx.waitForSignal(...)` that survive
deploys, and one [dashboard](/docs/durable/observability/dashboard) shows the end-to-end run. The authoring
convention is a deliberate parallel — `app/jobs` → `app/workflows`, `make:job` → `make:workflow`,
`Job.dispatch()` → `Workflow.dispatch(input)` — so moving a job over is mechanical (see
[From a queue job to a durable workflow](/docs/durable/authoring/app-workflows#from-a-queue-job-to-a-durable-workflow)).

Choose **a plain job** for fire-and-forget background work — a resize, an email, a webhook delivery — where
each job is independent and "workflow" would be overkill. Choose **`@adonis-agora/durable`** the moment a
process has *steps that must not re-run*, *waits*, or *state that outlives a deploy*.

## vs Temporal

Temporal is the reference implementation of durable execution, and its model — deterministic replay
of plain code — is the same one this library uses. If you've written a Temporal workflow,
`ctx.step` / `ctx.sleep` / signals / `ctx.patched` will all feel familiar; even the
[query/update split](/docs/durable/authoring/queries-and-updates) is deliberately Temporal-shaped. A few
things Temporal leaves as hand-rolled patterns are first-class here: [saga compensation](/docs/durable/reliability/sagas)
is a `{ compensate }` option on any step (undone in reverse, durably) instead of a try/catch you write
yourself, and [durable entities](/docs/durable/authoring/entities) — keyed, serialized actors — have no
Temporal equivalent at all.

The difference is operational. Temporal is a **separate orchestration cluster**: its own server,
its own database, its own deployment lifecycle, workers connecting over gRPC. That buys enormous
scale, multi-region namespaces, and battle-tested guarantees — and costs you a second distributed
system to run (or a Temporal Cloud bill). `@adonis-agora/durable` inverts the trade: the engine is a
provider in your app, state is rows in your existing database, and "deploying the orchestrator" is
just deploying your app.

Choose **Temporal** when workflow orchestration is a core, high-scale concern — millions of
concurrent runs, many teams, many languages — and a dedicated cluster earns its keep.
Choose **`@adonis-agora/durable`** when you're an AdonisJS shop that wants durable
checkout/onboarding/pipeline flows without adopting and operating a second platform.

## vs Inngest

Inngest attacks the same problem from the serverless direction: your steps are memoized function
invocations driven over HTTP by the Inngest server, which makes it a natural fit for
Vercel/Netlify-style deployments and event-driven fan-out. It is excellent at what it does, and its
managed offering removes the ops question entirely.

The differences that matter here: `@adonis-agora/durable` workflows are **`BaseWorkflow` classes built
by the AdonisJS container** — steps are `@Step` methods on injectable classes, tested like any other
service — and the suspend model parks a waiting run as a database row rather than re-invoking a function
per step. State stays in *your* database (a hard requirement in regulated or air-gapped environments), and
step dispatch rides your own [transport](/docs/durable/transports) (in-process, `@adonisjs/queue`, plain
DB polling, or BullMQ) instead of inbound HTTP. Inngest's signature event ergonomics have direct analogues
too: [`onEvent` with `eventBatch`](/docs/durable/authoring/events#coalescing-bursts--eventbatch)
starts workflows from published events with the same coalescing patterns ("reindex at most once the source
goes quiet", "process uploads in batches"), and [`@OnEvent` / `@OnDiagnostic`](/docs/durable/authoring/event-triggers)
bridge the Adonis emitter and diagnostics channels in without wiring.

Choose **Inngest** for serverless/edge architectures or when you want a managed platform with
event-driven ergonomics. Choose **`@adonis-agora/durable`** for long-running AdonisJS services,
container-first codebases, and data-residency constraints.

## vs BullMQ

BullMQ gives you jobs and queues: retries, priorities, rate limits, delayed jobs, and flows for
parent/child trees. Like a plain queue job, it has no replay, so the same limits apply: a multi-step
process is a chain of handlers passing state through job data, and long waits need a persistence scheme
of your own.

In AdonisJS you'd usually meet BullMQ through `@adonisjs/queue`, which is this library's primary
cross-process transport. A dedicated `transports.bullmq(...)` driver also exists for a specific reason:
it is byte-compatible with the NestJS `nestjs-durable` BullMQ transport and its Python worker, so an
AdonisJS engine and NestJS or [Python](/docs/durable/python) workers can share one Redis.

Choose **plain BullMQ** for independent background jobs where "workflow" would be overkill. Choose
**`@adonis-agora/durable`** — on whichever transport you already run — once a process has steps that must
not re-run, waits, or state that outlives a deploy.

## Where @adonis-agora/durable is honestly weaker

- **Scale ceiling.** The engine writes checkpoints to your SQL database. That's a feature (one
  source of truth, your backup story) until your run volume makes checkpoint writes a hot spot —
  a dedicated cluster like Temporal moves that ceiling far higher.
- **Ecosystem maturity.** Temporal has a decade of production hardening and a large community;
  this library is younger and AdonisJS-scoped by design.
- **Languages.** Cross-language support today means TypeScript workflows with Python workers (or
  Python-authored workflows over the same protocol) — not the per-language SDK breadth of Temporal.

If those are your constraints, use the bigger tool. If they aren't, skipping a second platform is
a real simplification — in code, in ops, and in the audit story.
